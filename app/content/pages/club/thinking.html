Cosmopolitan Thinking

What do Blue Citizenship and cosmopolitan thinking have in common? Both begin with the recognition that we live in a deeply interconnected world in which many of the most pressing challenges transcend national borders. Climate change, ocean degradation, migration, wars, global inequality, and resource conflicts cannot be understood – or addressed – through national perspectives alone. Blue Citizenship approaches this reality through the example of the ocean: a shared, life-sustaining physical feature that connects continents, societies and generations. Cosmopolitan thinking expands this perspective beyond the ‚blue‘ dimension and asks more broadly how the concept of citizenship could be developed in a way that lives up to the challenges of our time and the realitiy of our global interconnectedness. For us, cosmopolitanism is therefore not a rejection of local identies and national communities. Rather, it complements them by widening the horizon of political responsibility when dealing with obvious cosmopolitan issues – like the ocean. This includes the expansion of existing legal spaces so that global citizenship could be based on equal rights, an accessible sphere of civic engagement and forms of participation to actually shape the legal space you inhabit as a citizen. Thus, cosmopolitan thinking not only contributes to the discussion of identity and responsibility in an interconnected world, but raises real institutional questions. Could a global citizen be granted participatory rights in addition to their rights as a national citizen? Could Human Rights one day turn into binding cosmopolitan law? Does a global citizen need genuine parliamentary representation? Just to name a few… We’re trying to draw inspiration from the rich tradition and diverse contemporary forms of cosmopolitanism and understand the perspective of global citizenship as something that has to be developed as an intercultural and transgenerational endeavour. In a world of fierce geopolitics and growing nationalism, cosmopolitan thinking is needed more than ever.
